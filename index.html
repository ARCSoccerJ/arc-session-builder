Pick a tool, then click the pitch to place tokens — new players face the ball's starting spot by default, and each of the first 4 on a step gets its own color. Use Rotate and drag a player to change which way they're facing, and Foot then click a player's foot to mark which one they should receive with — click it again to clear it. Drop the ball onto a player's foot and it sticks there, following that player as you move or rotate them, until you grab the ball again with Move. Add a step to copy the current positions, drag tokens to their next spot, then hit Play to watch the drill run. Reset ball snaps the ball on this step back to bottom-center and clears its gate colors and receiving-foot marks, without touching your other steps. Click a gate on the boundary to cycle it through yellow, green, blue, and off — gate colors are saved per step, so they can change as the drill progresses. The Session fields above name the drill and record its type, focus, tags, gate timing, gates selected, and notes — this shows in a panel beside the pitch throughout any exported GIF. Every exported GIF is also saved to your Library, where you can tag it and filter by tag — each saved session carries its editable data too, so hitting Load on one (or Load Drill in the header, for a saved file) brings the whole thing back onto the board to branch into a new variation. Use Training Plan to lay out multiple days of sessions pulled from the Library — filter the picker by tag, and hit View next to any session to preview it before choosing — prescribe sets and balls for each, add notes, then export as a printable PDF (with every session's type, focus, tags, gate setup, and notes included, plus a QR code straight to each session's own GIF whenever it came from the shared Library) or as a standalone web page with the actual GIFs animating. Hit Save to Plans Library to store the plan itself — find it later in Plans Library, where anyone with the passphrase can open it to record scores, while full editing stays reserved for signed-in team.
